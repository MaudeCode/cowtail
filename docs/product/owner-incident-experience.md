# Owner Incident Experience

## Status

Accepted product direction. This document is the durable source for the alert, incident, Ranch, Roundup, and owner-action work discussed for Cowtail. It should be converted into Plane work items without changing the product intent.

## Product objective

Cowtail should behave as an owner-facing incident commander, not an alert database.

Its normal operating loop is:

```text
Signal → Investigate → Repair → Verify → Report
                    ↘ Ask Owner
```

Cowtail should autonomously complete this loop whenever a repair is bounded, reversible, and supported by evidence. It should interrupt the owner only when:

- a repair carries meaningful risk;
- required information is missing;
- investigation is genuinely blocked;
- a recurring condition needs a policy decision; or
- the system remains unhealthy after attempted repair.

The app should primarily show exceptions to automation, not every event generated by automation.

## Questions every incident must answer

The owner should be able to understand these within seconds:

1. What is affected?
2. How bad is it now?
3. What did Cowtail discover?
4. What has Cowtail already done and verified?
5. What decision, if any, is required?

Raw alert names, worker phases, attempt counts, and monitoring metadata are supporting evidence. They are not the primary story.

## Information architecture

The native app uses four first-class destinations:

1. **Inbox**: decisions, active incidents, and work requiring attention.
2. **Ranch**: current operational health across the whole environment.
3. **Roundup**: outcomes, trends, recurrence, and historical context.
4. **Farmhouse**: settings, identity, notifications, appearance, and developer options.

Farmhouse remains the settings page. Cluster health must not be moved into it.

## Inbox: a decision queue

The Inbox should open on work that matters now.

### Sections

#### Needs a decision

Show incidents that are blocked on an owner choice. Each card must include:

- plain-language incident title;
- affected service or capability;
- current impact;
- age;
- Cowtail's recommendation;
- the single primary decision.

Example:

```text
STORAGE AVAILABILITY AT RISK                         8m
3 placement groups remain undersized
Cowtail recommends restarting one OSD.
Impact: Data available, redundancy degraded

[Review proposed repair]
```

#### Cowtail is working

Show active investigation, repair, or verification in compact cards. Progress should describe useful work, not worker internals.

```text
PLEX PLAYBACK DEGRADED                              2m
Checking storage latency and active streams
Investigation 3 of 5 checks complete
```

#### Recently handled

Use dense rows for work that no longer requires attention:

```text
✓ Certificate renewed and verified                  18m
↺ Node pressure recovered without intervention      42m
⊘ Backup warning dismissed as expected maintenance   1h
```

### Inbox rules

- Rank by impact, required decision, and age rather than timestamp alone.
- Do not give every alert a large card.
- Do not repeat the same meaning with multiple badges such as “Needs You,” “Needs review,” and “Escalated.”
- Prefer human impact over raw Prometheus identifiers.
- Group related signals into one incident.
- Keep completed work compact and secondary.
- Remove the full-size cluster health card and routine metric tiles from the Inbox.
- When Ranch is degraded, show only a compact, tappable banner that links to Ranch.
- When Ranch is healthy, show no health banner.

## Ranch: current operational health

Ranch is a dedicated first-class page. It is operational, but it should not become a miniature Grafana dashboard.

### Overall state

Show one concise assessment with a last-refresh timestamp and pull-to-refresh:

```text
Cluster degraded
Storage redundancy needs attention. Compute and networking are healthy.
```

### Needs attention

Show only unhealthy, degraded, or uncertain systems, ranked by impact. Link active conditions to their incident.

```text
STORAGE                                         DEGRADED
3 placement groups undersized
Incident active for 18 minutes

[View incident]
```

### Systems

Use compact rows with plain-language state and the one most useful metric:

```text
Nodes              5/5 ready
Storage            Degraded · 68% used
Ingress            Healthy
Certificates       Healthy · next expiry 41d
Backups             Healthy · last run 3h ago
```

Initial system groups:

- Kubernetes nodes;
- Ceph and storage capacity;
- networking and ingress;
- certificates;
- core databases;
- backups;
- important services.

Tapping a row opens a system-specific detail page with:

- current state and impact;
- active and recent incidents;
- relevant resources;
- recent changes;
- useful external dashboard links;
- technical evidence on demand.

### Recent activity

Provide a compact operational timeline for repairs, recoveries, degradations, and successful routine work. These events provide context without becoming Inbox decisions.

### Naming

Use themed language sparingly. “Ranch” is the page name. “The Herd” may label nodes if it remains immediately understandable. Health states and technical facts stay plain and precise.

## Incident detail: an owner brief

The incident detail hierarchy must be:

1. current impact;
2. findings;
3. completed actions;
4. verification result;
5. recommended next step;
6. technical evidence and metadata.

### Header

Lead with a human title:

> Storage redundancy is degraded

Keep the source identifier secondary:

> `CephPlacementGroupsDegraded · storage`

### Current impact

Explain what users or systems experience now, including residual risk.

### What Cowtail found

Summarize the likely cause, supporting evidence, and confidence. Distinguish fact from inference.

### What Cowtail did

List actions already taken. Do not make the owner infer this from generic fix records.

### Verification

State what was checked after repair and whether the condition is actually healthy, improved, unchanged, or worse.

“Command succeeded” is not verification.

### Recommendation

Explain the proposed next action, why it is needed, its risk, its reversibility, and why Cowtail cannot safely proceed without approval.

### Decision bar

Use one contextual, thumb-reachable primary action and a small number of relevant alternatives. Keep it visible while reviewing the incident.

Example:

```text
[Approve restart]       [Hold for 1 hour]
               View evidence
```

### Evidence and diagnostics

Move raw alert payloads, labels, worker attempts, probe output, timestamps, and dashboard links into a collapsed evidence section. Preserve the audit trail without making it the main interface.

## Incident trail

Every incident should have a concise, durable trail that explains the automation loop:

```text
● Signal      9:12 PM
● Diagnose    OSD 7 flapping
● Repair      Restart attempted
● Verify      Still degraded
◉ Decision    Host restart requires approval
```

Trail event types should cover:

- signal received or updated;
- investigation started, completed, or blocked;
- finding established;
- repair proposed, started, completed, or failed;
- verification started and completed;
- owner decision requested and recorded;
- incident resolved, dismissed, snoozed, or reopened.

The trail replaces several disconnected status cards. It must remain readable without exposing worker implementation details.

## Contextual owner actions

Actions depend on incident state. Do not show a generic action panel for every incident.

| Situation | Primary action | Secondary actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investigation failed transiently | Retry investigation | View evidence, dismiss occurrence |
| Repair proposed and approval required | Approve repair | Hold, reject with note |
| Condition understood but non-actionable | Dismiss occurrence | Snooze rule, tune future handling |
| Cowtail is actively working | None | Follow progress, cancel only when safe |
| Repair completed and verified | None | View trail |
| Repeated noisy signal | Tune this alert | Dismiss once, snooze |
| Manual follow-up required | Create follow-up | Open relevant dashboard |

### Required action changes

- Remove **Keep in Needs You**. It describes a bucket rather than a decision.
- Rename retry actions in owner language. Do not mention durable queues or worker machinery.
- Do not style retry as destructive.
- Split **Mark Noise** into explicit choices:
  - dismiss this occurrence;
  - snooze this signal for a duration;
  - change how future occurrences are handled.
- Make the consequences and scope of each action clear before execution.
- Record owner notes and decisions in the incident trail.

## Incidents, not webhook deliveries

The primary product object should be an incident, not an individual alert delivery.

### Grouping behavior

- Correlate related signals that share a cause or affected capability.
- Firing and resolved updates modify the same incident lifecycle.
- Attach investigations, repairs, verification, and owner decisions to the incident.
- Keep raw signals available as evidence.
- Reopen or link recurring incidents rather than creating unrelated copies.
- Show recurrence, for example: “Third occurrence in seven days.”

Examples:

- Multiple Ceph degradation alerts become one storage degradation incident.
- Several playback alerts caused by the same storage latency event become one service-impact incident with supporting signals.

### Durable identity

Convex remains the source of truth. Each logical incident needs one durable lifecycle record, with events as append-only audit history. Correlation must be explicit and testable rather than depending on display-time grouping alone.

## Notification policy

Push notifications follow owner attention, not raw signal volume.

### Notify immediately

- Owner approval is required.
- Automation failed and impact remains.
- A repaired incident regressed.
- Critical impact cannot be safely mitigated.

### Put in Roundup

- Automatically repaired and verified.
- Self-resolved.
- Informational signals.
- Expected maintenance noise.
- Routine retries that eventually succeeded.

Notification copy must describe impact and required action:

```text
Storage redundancy remains degraded
Cowtail found a flapping OSD and recommends restarting its host. Approval required.
```

Do not lead with a raw alert identifier.

## Roundup: outcomes and patterns

Roundup should answer:

- What broke?
- What did Cowtail fix and verify?
- What recovered without intervention?
- What keeps recurring?
- What still needs follow-up?
- Did an owner decision change future handling?

Organize it as:

1. **Still needs attention**
2. **Fixed and verified**
3. **Recovered without intervention**
4. **Patterns worth addressing**
5. **Dismissed or suppressed**

The patterns section should elevate recurrence and systemic trends. One statement that a condition occurred six times this week is more useful than six resolved rows.

## Visual direction

Keep Cowtail's existing palette, typography, and personality. Improve structure rather than layering on decoration.

- Use fewer large cards.
- Use dense rows for history and routine activity.
- Put human impact before technical identifiers.
- Use one semantic status color per incident.
- Reserve red for active danger or genuinely destructive decisions.
- Use blue for automated work, amber for waiting or degradation, and green only after verification.
- Make the decision bar thumb-reachable.
- Disclose technical data progressively.
- Make the incident trail the distinctive visual element.
- Support Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, Reduce Motion, sufficient contrast, and minimum touch targets.

## Data and protocol requirements

Add structured, versioned fields where they cross process boundaries. Avoid asking clients to derive product meaning from free-form notes.

Required concepts:

- incident identifier and correlation fingerprint;
- lifecycle state;
- affected service, capability, and resources;
- user-visible impact and residual risk;
- severity and urgency;
- findings with evidence and confidence;
- completed repair actions;
- verification status and evidence;
- recommended next action;
- action risk and reversibility;
- whether an owner decision is required;
- allowed contextual actions;
- timeline events;
- recurrence count and related incidents;
- suppression scope and expiration;
- relevant external links.

Worker secrets, claim tokens, raw internal errors, and private owner notes must not leak into public or generic projections.

## Refresh and background behavior

This product direction does not require chat or a general realtime conversation system.

The app should refresh:

- when foregrounded;
- after a push notification;
- after an owner action;
- through pull-to-refresh;
- periodically while an incident is actively changing.

Progress may be delivered as durable incident events. The UI should tolerate stale data, show last refresh time where relevant, and reconcile optimistic actions with authoritative server state.

## Delivery sequence

### Phase 1: Native experience using existing data

- Establish the four-tab navigation: Inbox, Ranch, Roundup, Farmhouse.
- Keep Farmhouse strictly focused on settings.
- Replace the Inbox dashboard layout with the decision queue.
- Add compact Ranch status and system rows using the current health endpoint.
- Rewrite owner-facing copy.
- Reorder incident detail around impact, findings, action, verification, and next step.
- Replace generic owner actions with state-specific actions.
- Add the incident trail using existing lifecycle data where possible.
- Collapse technical metadata and evidence.
- Add focused unit, snapshot, and UI-test coverage for hierarchy and action availability.

### Phase 2: Structured incident contract and backend

- Introduce durable incident identity and correlation.
- Add structured impact, findings, recommendation, risk, repair, and verification fields.
- Add typed trail events and contextual allowed actions.
- Add recurrence and related-incident data.
- Preserve alert deliveries as evidence.
- Update protocol schemas, Convex storage, HTTP projections, generated clients, fixtures, and migration behavior.
- Test privacy boundaries, lifecycle transitions, correlation, and backwards compatibility.

### Phase 3: Operational capability

- Add approval-gated repairs.
- Add hold, snooze, dismissal, and future-handling controls with explicit scope.
- Add incident grouping and recurrence detection.
- Add useful deep links to operational systems.
- Add push-driven refresh and durable progress events.
- Add policy learning from explicit owner decisions without silently broadening permissions.
- Add outcome-based notification routing and Roundup patterns.

## Plane work-item map

Create one parent work item for the owner incident experience and child work items for:

1. Native navigation and the Ranch tab.
2. Inbox decision queue.
3. Owner-oriented incident detail.
4. Incident trail component and event model.
5. Contextual owner actions and copy.
6. Dismiss, snooze, and future-handling semantics.
7. Durable incident identity and signal correlation.
8. Structured findings, impact, recommendation, repair, and verification contracts.
9. Notification policy and actionable push copy.
10. Roundup outcomes and recurrence patterns.
11. Ranch system detail views and operational links.
12. Accessibility, Dynamic Type, and mobile interaction validation.
13. Migration, fixture, protocol-generation, and end-to-end verification.

The parent should define the product objective and cross-cutting acceptance criteria. Child work items should remain independently testable and should reference this document rather than duplicating the entire rationale.

## Cross-cutting acceptance criteria

- The owner can identify impact and required action from the Inbox without opening every incident.
- Related signals do not create duplicate owner decisions.
- Incident detail clearly distinguishes findings, actions, and verification.
- Cowtail never claims success without verification evidence.
- No generic action is shown when it is not valid for the current state.
- Action scope and consequences are explicit.
- Technical evidence remains available without dominating the interface.
- Farmhouse remains settings-only.
- Ranch provides current health without becoming a raw metrics dashboard.
- Routine automated success is summarized rather than paged.
- All owner decisions and automated steps are durably auditable.
- Private notes and internal worker data remain private.
- The app remains useful after relaunch and without a continuous realtime connection.

## Explicit non-goals

- Reintroducing chat or a conversational timeline.
- Showing every monitoring event as a first-class Inbox card.
- Rebuilding Grafana inside the app.
- Moving cluster health into Farmhouse.
- Treating a successful command as proof that a repair worked.
- Letting Cowtail infer broad standing permission from one narrow owner approval.
